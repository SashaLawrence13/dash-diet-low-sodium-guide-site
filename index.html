DASH Diet: Low Sodium Guide support

DASH Diet: Low Sodium Guide helps you follow the DASH eating plan published by the NIH and keep a record of your blood pressure. It is not medical advice and does not replace your doctor. Do not change your medication based on anything in this app.

Common questions

Where do the targets come from?

The food-group servings and the two sodium levels (2,300 mg and 1,500 mg) are the NIH National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute DASH Eating Plan tables. Nutrient values come from USDA FoodData Central. Packaged foods scanned by barcode come from Open Food Facts. The blood pressure categories are the 2017 ACC/AHA guideline definitions.

Which calorie level should I pick?

The app does not count calories. The calorie level only sets how many servings of each food group the NIH table asks for. If you do not know, 2,000 is the level the DASH handout uses. Your clinician or a dietitian can tell you which fits you.

A food I eat is missing.

Scan its barcode if it is packaged, or add it as a custom food with the sodium from its label. Email me the name and I will consider it for the next update.

The app says my reading is Stage 1. What does that mean?

The app shows the category your numbers fall into under the 2017 ACC/AHA guideline, with the source. It cannot tell you what your reading means for you. If you are concerned about a reading, contact your clinician. If your reading is over 180/120 and you have symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, change in vision or difficulty speaking, the American Heart Association says to call 911.

How do I get my data out?

Share the 30-day report from the Report tab. Blood pressure readings can also be written to Apple Health.

Does the app need the internet?

No. It works fully offline. The only network use is sending a barcode number to Open Food Facts when you scan one.
